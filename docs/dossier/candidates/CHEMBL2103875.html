Why this candidate is on the list

Tier 1 Tier 1 — literature-supported

TRAMETINIB, a small molecule that exploits the RAF/MEK/ERK dependence, has been shown to have in vitro activity against mosaic variegated aneuploidy (MVA) cells. The evidence grade is in vitro, and the candidate ranks 119 with support from two channels: prior and proximity. The in vitro study indicates that TRAMETINIB can be effective against MVA cells, although the mechanism of action in non-transformed cells is not explicitly detailed in the provided literature. Given the transformed cancer cell context of most aneuploidy-selectivity evidence, caution is advised when applying this to MVA cells, which are not transformed.

Evidence against it

Contradicting evidence

The evidence for trametinib's repurposing in mosaic variegated aneuploidy is primarily in vitro, and the in vitro evidence is limited to one record. Additionally, there is one adverse direction record, which suggests potential toxicity or inefficacy. Given the lack of clinical and in vivo data, the evidence grade is weak, and the supporting channels are based on prior or proximity, which do not strongly indicate an aneuploidy-selective effect. Therefore, trametinib should not be shortlisted for this indication.
